How the tuner learns

Small experiments · measured decisions
  1. 1ObserveCollect fresh counters and check cooling, errors and limits.
  2. 2Try a small stepRequest a nearby clock or voltage within your saved bounds.
  3. 3Settle & measureWait, then compare a complete valid window with the round reference.
  4. 4Keep, reject or back offUse the selected goal and noise margin; safety takes priority.

Error checks can reject an unstable trial or baseline before its full comparison window finishes. Only fresh, distinct counter intervals from the current evaluation count; old pre-change intervals are excluded. An early rejection is not a completed performance measurement. Successful trials still need the full settling and observation periods. Held or monitored results retain immediate backoff on a fresh error-limit breach. Error-rejected settings are skipped only for the current run.

A rejected measured trial returns to the better measured point when safety headroom allows. If protective backoff reaches your minimum limit, the miner can pause, wait until it is cool enough, restore the original settings and resume normal mining with auto-tuning off. Real hardware faults still require resolution; automatic recovery does not clear them.

This is a local, bounded search, not a guaranteed global optimum. Normal search steps are {{ info.limits.frequencyStepMHz }} MHz or {{ info.limits.voltageStepMv }} mV. Safety backoff can be larger. Higher voltage can reduce timing errors only when power and temperature headroom allow it. Error recovery allows at most two voltage increases before requiring clock reduction; the total remains limited to six correction attempts until a complete clean measurement succeeds. Noisy comparisons need more evidence, not a claimed gain.

Continuous Off completes a bounded round and holds its result. Continuous On monitors for changed conditions and may reassess; it does not adjust constantly. Neither setting starts tuning after a reboot.

The controller runs on the miner, including when this page is closed. Disabling requests restoration of the original pre-tuning backup. Restoration waits if conditions are unsafe; it never forces settings through a shutdown fault or clears that fault. Restarting never automatically starts another tuning run.
